# Report Scam Calls or Texts

Here you can let us know if you have received SMS messages or calls from a
suspicious sender (typically from a foreign number). We investigate the numbers
and block them if necessary.

Generally, we encourage you not to click on links or similar from numbers you
do not recognize. Additionally, you should avoid providing personal information
over the phone unless it is in connection with calls you have initiated
yourself.

If you have questions regarding scam calls or texts, please call us at 72 100
100.

Please note that we can only help with scam calls and texts sent to
Telenor customers.

**If your number is being misused for spoofing**

Spoofing is calls from abroad masked with a genuine Danish phone number. If
you experience receiving many calls from people who say you have called them,
your number may have been misused by fraudsters for spoofing.

If that is the case, unfortunately, we do not have the ability to block your
number, as that would mean you would not be able to use your phone yourself. We
can instead give you a new phone number, but our experience is that fraudsters
usually only use a number for spoofing for 1-2 weeks. We therefore recommend
waiting, even though it can be very stressful while it is happening.

If your number is being misused for spoofing and you want a new
phone number, you can call us at 72 100 100.

## Questions and Answers

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a form of fraud where the scammer tries to trick you into
providing your personal information.

If, while searching the web, you see a message that your device has been
infected with a virus, or if you receive a call from someone claiming to be
from, for example, Telenor and asking for your account name and password, you
are likely the victim of a scam. Scammers use all means – fake emails, pop-up
messages, SMS messages and calls – to trick you into sharing your personal
information, such as your computer password, NemID key card or your credit
card details. Use the information in this writing to protect your account and
avoid scams.

**What to do if you receive a suspicious call or voicemail message**

Scammers manipulate phone numbers and use flattering or threatening language to
trick you into giving them information, NemID key card or credit card
information. Many of the phone calls typically come from English-speaking
people. If Danish companies were to call you, it would generally be in Danish.
If you receive a suspicious phone call, do not provide any personal information
and hang up. Always verify the identity of the person on the phone before
disclosing personal information. If you receive an unsolicited call from
someone claiming to be from, for example, Telenor and you are unsure, you can
hang up and contact the company directly.

**What to do if you receive a suspicious email or SMS**

Scammers try to copy emails and SMS messages from legitimate companies to trick
you into sharing your personal information and passwords. Many fraudulent
emails and fraudulent SMS messages are typically written in Danish, often not
natural Danish, typically translated using translation programs. Do not open
attachments and click on links in suspicious and unsolicited messages.

The following are signs of phishing attempts:

• The sender's email address or phone number does not match the name of the
company the sender claims to be from.

• The message was sent to an email address or phone number that you have not
given to the company.

• The message starts with an anonymous greeting, e.g. "Dear Customer". Most
legitimate companies use your name when contacting you.

• A link appears to be legitimate, but it leads to a website that does not
match the address of the company's website. Check the link by hovering your
mouse over the link to see the URL in the status bar / hold your finger down
on the link.

• The message looks very different from the ones you have received from the
company before.

• The message asks you to provide personal information such as a credit card
number or account password.

• The message was sent to you unsolicited and there is an attachment.

What is Wangiri?

Wangiri are scam calls that call you in the hope that you call back and are
thus charged a lot of money for the call.

**Tips on how to avoid becoming a victim of Wangiri scam calls**

Wangiri is the term for scam calls where the fraud takes place by a foreign
number calling you very briefly and hanging up before you can answer the call.
If you call back, you will often be connected to a voicemail in a foreign
country, and you will receive a large bill for the call because the number
charges a very high per-minute rate (is overcharged). You can avoid this type
of fraud by never calling back to foreign numbers you do not recognize.

3 good tips to avoid answering Wangiri scam calls:

• Check if it is a number you know before you call back.

• Check the number of digits. Remember that a real Danish number contains eight
digits and starts with '+45'

• If in doubt, do not call back. If it was important, the person will probably
call again.

What is Spoofing?

Spoofing is calls from abroad masked with a Danish phone number.

Spoofing is the term for a type of scam call where foreign fraudsters are, as
it were, "hiding" behind a Danish number. When you are called, it looks like
the call is coming from a Danish number, and when you answer the call, you
often get a foreign fraudster on the line who tries to trick you into giving
them personal information. This type of call can be difficult to protect
yourself against, and the owner of the Danish number, which is used for the
fraud, can experience it as a major nuisance, because many (those who are
exposed to the fraud) will call back to the number within a short time.

**Others say they are being called from my number**

If you experience that others say they have been called from your number, even
though you have not called them, you may be affected by spoofing. With
spoofing, the foreign fraudster has changed the display of his own number to a
random Danish number. In this way, it looks as if you are being called from a
Danish number even though the call is coming from abroad. The foreign
fraudsters do not take over the number. The Danish number is only displayed to
the recipients of the call.

**Why do fraudsters use Spoofing?**

The foreign fraudsters want to talk to Danes in order to trick information
about credit cards or the like out of them. The fraudsters know from
experience that many avoid answering calls from foreign numbers, and therefore
they want it to look as if they are calling from a Danish number. The
fraudsters typically claim that they can deliver a service you need. They just
need your payment information.

**What should you do if you are exposed to Spoofing?**

First and foremost, you should avoid providing personal information over the
phone unless it is in connection with calls you have initiated yourself. If
you want to protect yourself against spoofing, you can first and foremost avoid
answering calls from numbers you do not recognize. When people need to talk to
you, the most normal thing is that they leave a message or call you back.

**If your number is being used for Spoofing**

Telenor does not have the ability to block your number. If we did, you would
not be able to use your phone yourself, and you would not be able to receive
the calls you want on your phone. We know from experience that fraudsters
typically use a random number for spoofing for 1-2 weeks before switching to
another number. It can be very irritating during that period because you
receive many calls from others who have been called by the fraudsters. You are
always welcome to contact us if you have further questions about possible
spoofing fraud.
