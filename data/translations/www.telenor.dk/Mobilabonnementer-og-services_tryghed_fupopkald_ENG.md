# Scam Calls

There are many types of scam calls, and they can be difficult to see through. Some will try to get personal information so they can use your money or your identity. This is also called vishing (voice phishing). Others call from a foreign number with a very high per-minute rate. Scam calls originating from abroad often "hide" behind a Danish number.

## Have You Received a Scam Call?

**Report the call to us**

It is always important to react as quickly as possible if you have been subjected to a scam call. Let us know if you have received SMS messages or calls from a suspicious sender (typically from a foreign number). We investigate the numbers and block them if necessary.

## Good Advice to Avoid Being Scammed

  * Check if you recognize the number that is calling.
  * If in doubt, do not answer the call.
  * If you missed a call from a foreign number, do not call back.
  * If you answer a call and the caller hangs up, do not call back.
  * If someone wants to contact you, they will likely try again if it is important.
  * Are you expecting a call, for example, from a company or family abroad? Check which number they are calling from, either before or after a call.

## Do You Want to Know More?

Wangiri Calls

Wangiri is Japanese and can be translated as "ring and hang up." It is a form of scam call where a computer calls a large number of numbers and hangs up shortly after. For many, it is common courtesy to call back when they see they have missed a call. But if you call the number, you pay a high per-minute rate for the call, and it can become expensive.

Spoofing

Spoofing means to imitate or to deceive. Scammers who use spoofing call from a trustworthy Danish number, and the person on the other end claims to want to help you with a problem or offer you a service.

Spoofers pretend to be calling from, for example, your bank, a service you use, or a well-known business. You may be asked for personal information such as your CPR number, your MitID, or your credit card details. Your information is used for identity theft or to get access to your money.

Therefore, you should never give your personal information to someone who calls you – no matter where they say they are calling from. If you are unsure whether the person on the other end is really from the bank, ask for the person's name, hang up, and call the bank and ask for the person.

It is in our nature to be friendly to the person on the other end, but remember that an employee at a bank or another public authority will never ask for personal information over the phone – it is always okay to be skeptical and ask detailed questions.
