# Fraud Attempts

We give you tips and advice on what to do when you have received a dubious SMS
or email.

[Report Fraud Attempts](/kundeservice/svindel/#rapporter)[The Most Common
Fraud Schemes](/kundeservice/svindel/#svindelformer)

Have you received an email or SMS that seems suspicious? Are you wondering
how to distinguish a genuine email from a fake one? Here we give you some tips
on what to check, and the opportunity to report experienced fraud attempts.

## What should you do when you receive a suspect email or SMS?

**WARNING! We will never ask our customers to provide sensitive information in an email
or SMS**

### Take your time when reading emails or SMS

  * **Stop - Think - Check**

  * Ask yourself what the sender actually wants you to do. Are you expecting such a request? Is it something you usually get from the sender?

### Check the sender address of the email

  * In many cases, you will be able to reveal fraud by seeing that the sender address looks unserious.

  * Is it misspelled, or does it end in .com or telenor.ru (or other countries) instead of .no?

### Do not click on the link or attachments

  * If you are unsure about the content of the email or SMS, **do not click on the link or open attachments**

  * Instead, visit the official website of the sender by typing the address into the browser. If the content is genuine, you will usually find the same information there.

### Do not provide sensitive information

  * Do not provide sensitive information, card information or BankID details on websites you are routed to via links you are unsure about.

  * Feel free to go directly to the website in your browser (e.g. go directly to telenor.no, not via the link in the SMS/email)

### Email and SMS about invoice or refund

  * Check if you are actually entitled to a refund or if you have an unpaid invoice.

  * Visit your online bank and see if you have actually been charged twice or if there is an unpaid invoice there.

  * If you have paid the invoice to us twice or are entitled to a refund, you will **automatically be refunded** to the account you last paid from.

### Look carefully at the language and content

  * Often there will be spelling mistakes in the email or SMS, it is written in a threatening way and has a messy design.

  * Although the language in fake emails has gotten better and better, it often shows signs of having been translated via, for example, Google Translate.

### Discuss with others

  * Call a friend, a colleague or a family member.

  * Talk about the content of the email. Such a conversation will often be experienced as necessary and useful.

## Net Protection can save you from being scammed!

Regardless of what the scammers are after, their method often involves
sending you to a fake website that has been created to get your private
information.

Net Protection and Net Protection+ are security filters from Telenor that allow you to surf
a little safer.

### How the security filters Net Protection and Net Protection+ work

The security filters always check if the websites you are trying to visit are
unsafe. If you click on an unsafe website, the security filter will
block the attempt and stop you before you are scammed.

Even though Net Protection stops many fraud attempts, it is wise to take precautions
when you are online. You can read more about it and Net Protection+ by clicking on the button
below.

## Do you want to report fraud attempts?

Telenor Norway is taking a number of steps to combat digital crime. If
you receive a suspicious SMS or email, you can forward them to us via
one of the methods below. Through such reports, we can more quickly catch
spam/fraud attempts, and make an assessment of whether this should be blocked so that they
do not reach more people.

### Report suspicious SMS

  * Forward the message to 7726 (SPAM)
We will then assess whether the message has a fraudulent motive, and whether it should be
blocked.

  * With iPhone with version newer than iOS 17.4 you can let us know via the Messages app:

    * _If you have already opened the message:_
A **Report Junk** link appears at the bottom of all messages from unknown
senders. Tap **Report Junk**, and then tap **Delete** and
**Report Junk**.

    * _If you have not opened the message:_
**Swipe left** on the message - Tap the red trash can - Tap
then **Delete** and **Report Junk**.

### Report suspicious email

If you have received a suspicious email, experienced telephone fraud,
invoice fraud or similar, you can report it to us using the form below.

By you as a customer reporting messages you receive, we can more quickly catch
fraud attempts and make an assessment of whether these should be blocked in our
systems. We assess all reports we receive, but unfortunately cannot answer the messages we receive.
